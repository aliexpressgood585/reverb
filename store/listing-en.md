## Full description (4000 characters max)

```
Miss a jump and you don't fall. You freeze — at the highest point you reached, out in the middle of the gap you failed to cross.

And what you leave behind is solid.

CAIRN is a one-thumb climbing game where every death builds the tower. Drag to aim, release to launch, and watch the arc you drew become the arc you fly. Make it, and you climb. Miss, and your body becomes the step that the next attempt uses.

Climb long enough and the shaft below you is a monument made out of every version of yourself that didn't make it.

■ EVERY DEATH LEAVES A STONE

This is not a metaphor and it is not a scoring system. Your bodies are physical platforms. You stand on them. Some gaps are hard enough that throwing yourself into them deliberately — putting a body exactly where you need a step — is the correct move.

The game will show you the body you are about to leave, at the exact spot it will freeze, before you commit. Where you die is a decision.

■ ONE THUMB, NOTHING ELSE

Drag in the direction you want to go. That's the whole control scheme. No buttons, no virtual stick, no menus in the way. Pull further from your character and the aim gets finer, not stronger — so the hardest jumps in the game are aimed with the same thumb as the easiest ones.

Death to your next attempt is 900 milliseconds. There is nothing to dismiss.

■ THE TOWER IS YOURS

Nothing resets. The bodies you leave stay where you left them, forever, and the tower grows across every session you play. Old ones erode — they narrow, then they stop bearing weight, then they become gold outlines that are drawn but no longer hold you — so the tower keeps its history without ever becoming a staircase.

Pull back with two fingers to see all of it at once, from the base to your highest point, and share the image.

■ SOMETHING TO CLIMB TOWARD

The tower is not an endless corridor of ledges. Structures stand in it — a stair that collapsed, a lattice mast still lit at the top, a chain descending out of the dark — one to a region, and the first is above you in the opening view. You climb through them.

There are things in this tower nobody will tell you about. That is deliberate.

■ YOUR BEST RUN CLIMBS BESIDE YOU

The run that set your record replays as you go, and it moves when YOU move: at your seventh jump it stands exactly where your best self stood at its seventh. Not a stopwatch — a comparison of climbing, which is the only kind this game could honestly make. It runs out at your record height and leaves you at the frontier.

■ A DAILY CLIMB

One tower per day, derived from the date itself, identical for everyone on earth at the same moment. Send someone the date and they play your exact climb.

■ WHAT IS IN IT

· An endless tower with no ceiling — the difficulty keeps escalating and never flattens
· A daily challenge with its own separate save
· 30 marks to earn, most of them about the tower rather than about height
· Lifetime statistics: stones laid, distance climbed, times you stood on yourself
· A streak that counts consecutive days
· A share image of your whole tower
· Structures standing in the tower, one per region, and secrets in them
· Your record run, replaying beside you, jump for jump
· English and Hebrew, with full right-to-left layout

■ HOW IT WAS BUILT

Nothing in CAIRN was downloaded. Every sound is synthesised on the fly, every image is drawn in code, and the whole game is 37 kilobytes compressed. There are no loading screens because there is nothing to load.

No ads. No purchases. No currency, no shop, no timers, no energy, nothing to unlock with money. You are not the product here.

Plays offline, entirely.

■ ONE MORE THING

Nobody is going to tell you how to play this. There is no tutorial and no text prompt. The first jumps are wide enough that you cannot really miss them, and by the second death you will have worked out what the game is about.

That is the whole design.
```

## ASO notes, and what could not be researched

**What I could verify:** Play's own documentation, reachable from here.

**What I could not:** `play.google.com` is blocked by this environment's egress
proxy, so I could not look at what actually ranks in the
hyper-casual / minimalist-arcade category today. **The keyword choices below are
reasoning, not research, and should be checked against a real store before
launch.** That is a genuine gap and the brief asked for research rather than
guesses.

The reasoning that shaped the copy:

- **Long-tail over head terms.** "Arcade game" and "casual game" are unwinnable
  for a new listing with no installs. Terms like *climbing game*, *one thumb*,
  *minimalist*, *offline*, *no ads*, *daily challenge* are what a listing like
  this can actually place on, and every one of them appears in the description
  naturally.
- **"Offline" and "no ads" are searched as filters, not as features.** They are
  the two most common qualifiers appended to casual-game searches, they are both
  true here, and they are both stated in plain words rather than in a bullet.
- **The first two lines are the whole listing.** Play truncates the description
  at roughly three lines on mobile before "read more". Those lines are the
  mechanic, stated concretely — not a genre label.
- **The mechanic is the keyword.** Nothing else in this category freezes you
  where you died and builds a platform out of it. "Climb on what you were" is
  worth more than any generic term because it is the only phrase this game can
  own outright.
